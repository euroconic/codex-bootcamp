# Mission 03 — Market Research

> Mentor: works with or without internet. If the learner has web access, they can research live. If not, the seed notes in data/competitors.md are enough to produce a real brief. Do not block on connectivity.

## Goal
Produce a market research brief for Signal: who the competitors are, where the gap is, and where Signal should position itself.

## Why it matters
Founders fall in love with ideas and skip the question "does anyone else already do this, better?" Good market research is not about confirming your idea, it is about finding the gap your idea can own. This is what separates a fundable product from a hobby.

## What to do
1. Start from `data/competitors.md` (rough internal notes already gathered).
2. Ask your mentor to build the brief. A strong prompt:
   > "Using data/competitors.md (and web research if available), write a market research brief in workspace/market-research.md covering: the main competitors and their weakness, the real status quo we're fighting, where the gap is, and a recommended positioning for Signal in one sentence. Be specific, not generic."
3. Force a point of view. Ask: "where exactly should Signal play: up-market vs the underserved self-serve gap? Pick one and defend it." If you are not sure which way to lean, ask your mentor "which is the safer bet for a 6-person startup, and why?" then make sure the brief actually commits instead of hedging.
4. If you have web access, ask it to verify or expand the competitor list and pricing.

## Definition of Done
- `workspace/market-research.md` exists.
- It names competitors with their specific weakness, identifies the real status quo (hint: the manual spreadsheet), states where the gap is, and commits to a one-sentence positioning for Signal.
- Fail condition (mentor: hold them here if so): a brief that lists options without choosing one does NOT pass. The positioning must commit.

## Hints (mentor: one at a time)
1. The most important competitor is not a company, it is "a spreadsheet someone tags by hand once and abandons." Make sure that shows up.
2. A brief without a recommendation is just a list. Push for the positioning sentence.
3. If web research is available, pricing and "self-serve vs sales-led" are the most useful things to verify.

## What you just learned
Market research is not a literature review, it is a hunt for the gap plus a committed point of view. You can go from rough notes to a defensible positioning in one sitting.
