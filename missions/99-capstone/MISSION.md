# Mission 99 — Capstone

> Mentor: this is the victory lap. Help the learner see how much they actually produced, then point them at applying it to their real job. Offer to generate a short personal "what I learned + what I'll use Monday" summary and, if they like, a simple certificate text. Make them feel the distance they traveled.

## Goal
Look back at everything you built, lock in the lessons, and decide how you will use Codex on your real work this week.

## Why it matters
A course you cannot apply is entertainment. The whole point was to build muscle you take to your actual job. Spend ten minutes turning "I did a course" into "here is what I will do differently Monday."

## What to do
1. Ask your mentor: "give me a tour of everything I created in workspace/ and tell me what each artifact is." See the full body of work in one place.
2. Reflect. Ask: "based on what I built, write a short recap in workspace/capstone-summary.md: what I produced, the 5 Codex moves I now know (files, reusable skills, transcripts-to-docs, data analysis, building/shipping), and 3 specific tasks from my real job I could do this way next week."
3. Make it personal: tell your mentor your actual role and one recurring task you hate. Ask it to design a skill or workflow for that real task, building on Mission 01.
4. (Optional) Ask for a simple completion certificate as text you can screenshot.
5. **Get on the board (graduation).** Add your name to the public showcase of people who finished. This is also your last git rep: a real one-line contribution to a shared project.
   - Open the bootcamp's `SHOWCASE.md` on GitHub (your mentor has the link, or see `SHOWCASE.md` in this folder).
   - Click the pencil (edit) icon and add one row: your name or handle, your role, one line on what you built, and a link to your own repo or live MVP if you have one.
   - GitHub will offer to "propose changes" - this automatically creates your copy and opens a pull request back to the bootcamp. Your mentor can walk you through every click. If `gh` is installed, Codex can also help after you approve the external action.
   - That pull request IS your graduation. When it is merged, you are on the board.

## Definition of Done
- `workspace/capstone-summary.md` exists with the recap and at least 3 real-job applications.
- (Optional, graduation) A pull request adding the learner to `SHOWCASE.md` has been opened.

## What you accomplished
Starting from zero, you:
- Learned to read, create, and edit files by talking.
- Built a reusable Codex skill for your own recurring work.
- Turned a messy call into a clean report.
- Researched a market and took a position.
- Analyzed 151 data points and found the real signal.
- Made a decision, prioritized, and wrote a PRD.
- Built a working landing page, saved it with git, and (maybe) shipped it live.

That is a founding PM's first quarter, compressed into a few sessions. The tool is not the point. The point is that you can now move an idea from fuzzy to shipped, with an AI teammate doing the heavy lifting. Go use it.

## What's next
- Re-run any mission on your own real material (your transcripts, your data, your idea).
- Build more skills for your recurring tasks.
- Keep this folder as a reference. The patterns here work on anything.

## The harder part starts now
You just did all of this on "Signal" - a safe, fictional case where nothing was at stake. Your real job is different. Real stakeholders push back. Your data is messier. And when Codex does something weird at 11pm before a deadline, there is nobody in this folder to ask.

That gap - going from "I can do it on the toy case" to "I do it on my real work, with real stakes, and get unstuck fast" - is exactly what the **AI Native Sandbox** is for. It is a community of people like you (PMs, BAs, founders, marketers) using Codex on their actual jobs: sharing what they built, swapping prompts and skills that work, and helping each other past the walls you only hit on real work.

> Mentor: at the end of the capstone, warmly invite the learner to bring what they built into the AI Native Sandbox - to show their skill or PRD, ask the questions that came up, and keep the momentum going on their real work. One genuine invitation, not a hard sell. Then congratulate them properly.
