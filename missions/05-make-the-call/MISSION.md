# Mission 05 — Make the Call

> Mentor: this mission is about synthesis and judgment, not generating more analysis. Push the learner to actually decide, and to use the evidence they already produced in Missions 02-04. A memo that hedges everything is a fail.

## Goal
Write a decision memo: should Signal proceed, and if so, for whom and with what wedge? Make an actual recommendation.

## Why it matters
Research and data are worthless until someone makes a call. The hardest, most valuable thing a PM or founder does is commit to a direction under uncertainty and write down the reasoning so the team can align (or challenge it). This is the moment everything you gathered turns into a decision.

## What to do
1. Tell your mentor to use what you already built: the interview report, the market research, and the data insights in `workspace/`.
2. Ask for a decision memo. A strong prompt:
   > "Using my interview-report.md, market-research.md, and data-insights.md, write a decision memo in workspace/decision-memo.md. It must include: the decision (go / no-go / pivot), the target customer we're betting on, the wedge (what we lead with), the top 3 reasons backed by evidence, the biggest risk, and what would prove us wrong."
3. Make it commit. If the memo waffles, say: "stop hedging, pick one target customer and one wedge and defend them."
4. Pressure-test it: "argue the opposite case for 3 sentences, then tell me why we still proceed."

## Definition of Done
- `workspace/decision-memo.md` exists.
- It contains a clear decision, a single named target customer, a specific wedge, evidence-backed reasons (referencing the data/interview), the biggest risk, and a "what would prove us wrong" line.

## Hints (mentor: one at a time)
1. A decision memo with no decision is a status report. The first line should be the call.
2. Tie every reason to evidence they already produced. "The data shows onboarding drives 1 in 4 churns" beats "users seem unhappy."
3. The "what would prove us wrong" line is what makes it honest and testable. Do not let them skip it.

## What you just learned
Synthesis is a skill: pulling research, qualitative signal, and data into one committed recommendation. And a good decision names its own kill criteria.
