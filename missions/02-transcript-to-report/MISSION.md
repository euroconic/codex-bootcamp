# Mission 02 — Transcript to Report

> Mentor: the learner now uses real source material for the first time. The win is "I fed it a mess and got a clean document out." Encourage them to actually read the transcript a little so they appreciate how messy it is.

## Goal
Turn the messy discovery call in `data/interview-call.txt` into a clean, structured interview report a teammate could read in two minutes.

## Why it matters
Raw calls, meetings, and interviews are where the truth lives, but nobody re-listens to a 30-minute recording. The PMs and founders who win are the ones who turn raw conversations into shareable insight fast. This is the single most common "AI saved me an hour" task in real product work.

## What to do
1. Skim `data/interview-call.txt` so you see how messy a real transcript is (tangents, "um", half-thoughts).
2. Ask your mentor to read it and produce a structured report. A strong prompt:
   > "Read data/interview-call.txt and write an interview report with: a one-paragraph summary, the customer's top pain points, the current workaround they use, what they said they would pay for, dealbreakers, and 3 notable quotes. Save it to workspace/interview-report.md."
3. Read the result and push back if anything is vague: "the pain points are too generic, tie each one to something Derek actually said." A good pain point names something Derek really said (a number, a tool, a story). If it could apply to any company, it is too generic, send it back.
4. Before you call it done, ask: "is there anything important in the transcript you think you missed, or anything you're not fully sure you got right?" This is a habit worth keeping for life: a first draft from an AI is a draft, not a verdict, and asking it to double-check itself against the source often surfaces something.
5. (Optional) Ask for a Word version: "also save this as a Word document" and your mentor will hand you a .docx.

## Definition of Done
- `workspace/interview-report.md` exists.
- It includes at minimum: a summary, pain points, the current workaround, willingness to pay, dealbreakers, and real quotes drawn from the transcript (not invented).

## Hints (mentor: one at a time)
1. The best reports quote the source. Push them to ask for direct quotes.
2. If the report feels generic, the fix is a better prompt: name the exact sections you want.
3. Point out the gold in the transcript: the "two weeks of manual tagging" story, "wrong is worse than nothing", "connect and see value same day". A good report surfaces those.

## What you just learned
You can compress any raw conversation into structured, shareable insight in under a minute, and you can direct the output by naming the sections you want. Garbage in, structure out.
