# The Case: Signal

This is the world you operate in for the whole bootcamp. Read it once. Every mission happens inside this story.

---

## Your role

You are the **founding Product Manager at Signal**, a 6-person startup. You joined three weeks ago. The founders are smart but moving on gut feel, and they have asked you to bring some rigor before they spend the next six months building.

## The idea

Companies drown in customer feedback. It arrives everywhere: support tickets, sales calls, app store reviews, Slack messages, NPS surveys, churn emails. Nobody has time to read all of it, so the loudest voice or the most recent complaint wins. Real patterns get missed.

**Signal's bet:** a tool that ingests all that scattered feedback and turns it into a short, ranked list of "what to build next, and why." Think of it as a product manager's radar.

## The problem you have been handed

The founders believe in the idea. But "companies drown in feedback" is a hunch, not a validated problem. Before Signal commits engineering time, you need to answer real questions:

- Is this a painful, frequent, expensive problem, or just a nice-to-have?
- Who feels it most? Founders? PMs at mid-size SaaS? Support leads?
- What do people use today, and why does it fall short?
- If we build it, what is the smallest version that proves the value?
- Is it worth building at all?

## What you have to work with

In the `data/` folder you have been given the raw material a real PM would start with:

- **`interview-call.txt`** — a transcript of a messy 30-minute discovery call with a potential customer. Unstructured, full of tangents, like every real call.
- **`feedback.csv`** — an export of ~150 pieces of feedback from a friendly design-partner company that agreed to share their data. This is your dataset to analyze.
- **`competitors.md`** — rough notes on the existing tools in this space.
- **`personas-brief.md`** — background on the kinds of people who might buy Signal.

## Your job across the bootcamp

You will take Signal from a fuzzy hunch to a defensible, shipped artifact:

1. Build yourself a reusable Codex skill to handle your recurring busywork.
2. Turn that messy call into a clean, shareable interview report.
3. Research the market and the competition.
4. Analyze the feedback data to find the real patterns.
5. Make a clear go / no-go / pivot decision.
6. Prioritize what to build first.
7. Write the PRD.
8. Build a landing page MVP to test demand.
9. Save your work properly.
10. Put it live on the internet.

By the end you will have done, in miniature, exactly what a founding PM does in their first quarter. And you will have learned to do all of it with an AI assistant at your side.

Welcome to Signal. Let's find out if this thing is real.
